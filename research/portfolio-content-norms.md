# Portfolio content norms: what strong engineer sites include beyond projects

Research for issue #55. Date: 2026-10-20. Scope: what exemplar personal sites of ML/AI and platform engineers (and well-regarded engineer sites generally) carry beyond a project list, and what hiring-side commentary says gets read. This note does not decide anything; the personal content and writing tickets decide.

Confidence key: **[P]** page fetched and read today; **[S]** secondary or vendor advice (SEO-style blogs; treat as opinion); **[A]** anecdotal or unverified figure.

## Evidence base and its limits

- Exemplar pages fetched today [P]: karpathy.ai, huyenchip.com, eugeneyan.com, simonwillison.net/about, lilianweng.github.io, jvns.ca (home and /about), joshwcomeau.com, brittanychiang.com, kalzumeus.com/about, sive.rs/now, nownownow.com/about, uses.tech.
- Hiring-side commentary is thin and mostly [S]. Most "what hiring managers want" posts are vendor or bootcamp content. One page cites a "73% of hiring managers" Stack Overflow 2024 figure that could not be confirmed; ignore it. A "93% of 60+ hiring managers would look at a portfolio if given, but 51% say chances do not drop without one" figure appears in an aggregator [A].
- No source found documents hiring managers penalizing oversharing; that is inference from the general advice to stay brief and engineering-focused.
- Fetch summaries come from a small model; counts and dates below are as reported and worth a glance before being quoted publicly.

## Per-element findings

### About section or page and length

- Exemplars keep the bio short, 2 to 3 sentences up top: Karpathy (three-sentence bio inside a long page of timeline, talks, writing), Huyen (about two paragraphs, credentials inline), Yan (about three sentences, repeated in the footer), Julia Evans /about (two short paragraphs plus a topic list), Kalzumeus /about (about 400 words, witty). [P]
- Longer About pages do a different job: Simon Willison's /about is a conference bio plus disclosures, feeds, and how the site is built (the longest section is conflict-of-interest disclosure). That suits a prolific writer, not a landing-page reader. [P]
- Hiring-side guidance: landing intro of 1 to 3 sentences on what you work on; About is a short bio, current role, specific interests; avoid "passionate about everything". No word count is given anywhere. ([techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)) [S]
- Another guide warns that About pages "sound like a resume generator" and recommends warm, specific, first-person facts. ([MentorCruise](https://mentorcruise.com/blog/how-to-build-a-portfolio-that-gets-hiring-managers-to-care/)) [S]

### Personal touches (pets, hobbies, photos)

- Common among exemplars, but small and specific, not a section: Brittany Chiang's About names climbing, tennis, her wife and two cats, and Zelda; the footer has a clickable TARDIS GIF. Karpathy lists sci-fi reading, past speedcubing, a joke "Order of the Unicorn" paragraph, and a "zero frameworks" footer note. Huyen mentions growing up in a rice-farming village and her Vietnamese story collections. Evans names Montreal and the Recurse Center. Weng links Instagram drawings. [P]
- The memorable ones tie the touch to craft or voice (Karpathy's minimal-site boast, Huyen's storytelling and book translations, Evans's exclamation-point topic list) rather than listing pets for their own sake.
- Counter-advice for hiring-oriented sites: "travel photos and hobby projects generally don't help hiring"; the exception is non-engineering work that shows relevant skill (technical YouTube channel, published book). ([techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)) [S]. Other guides say a few hobbies in the bio are fine. ([Codecademy](https://www.codecademy.com/resources/blog/what-to-include-in-a-junior-developer-portfolio)) [S]
- The same source notes backend, infrastructure, and data recruiters rarely visit personal sites, which matters for this owner's audience: the site is more likely opened by the engineer vetting depth than by the first-pass recruiter. [S]

### /now and /uses pages

- /now is a real, small movement: Derek Sivers runs nownownow.com, listing sites with a /now page ("what you'd tell a friend you hadn't seen in a year"). Sivers's own /now is about 600 to 700 words, date-stamped ("Updated October 15th, 2026"), mixing travel, music, family, and programming notes. [P]
- /uses is a directory (uses.tech) of developer setup pages, roughly 950 entries by its own tag. [P]
- Among the ML/AI exemplars fetched, none showed a visible /now or /uses: Yan's homepage has none, and eugeneyan.com/uses returned 404. Both are therefore a generalist-blogger and indie-web convention, not an ML-hiring norm. [P]
- Both go stale: the maintenance advice is a quarterly 15-minute review, and "an outdated site hurts more than none". [S]

### Writing and cadence

- Writing is the dominant "beyond projects" element at the ML/AI exemplars: Huyen (blog is the primary nav item, books), Yan (Start Here, Writing, Speaking, Prototyping; about 212 posts, 31 talks, 19 prototypes, newsletter of 11,800+), Weng (blog only, 20 to 40 minute posts), Willison (daily output, six content types, per-type feeds), Evans (about one post per 29 days, bursty). [P]
- Cadence among them is irregular, not metronomic: Yan roughly one post per 1 to 2 months with a five-month gap; Weng gaps of months to over a year; Evans gaps up to about 68 days. Depth and quality, not frequency, carry reputation. [P]
- Hiring-side: "3 strong posts beat 30 weak ones"; a "Selected writing" list of 3 to 5 if you write occasionally; stale blogs and "Coming Soon" sections are named as things to skip. ([techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)) [S]
- Blog advice for general professionals ("at least monthly") conflicts with how the technical exemplars actually publish; it is not engineer-specific. [S]

### Talks

- Appear when the person has them, as a curated handful: Karpathy a thumbnail grid with event and year; Yan five featured talks; Huyen a short mention linking an events page; Evans "sometimes". Willison's About lists none, pointing to a conference bio. [P]
- Absent entirely from Chiang's page. No source suggests an empty Talks section; the advice is to include only strong ones. [S]

### Testimonials

- None of the exemplars fetched shows testimonials (Comeau explicitly none on the home page). Social proof there is reader counts, book mentions, employer names, and stats rows (Yan: posts, talks, prototypes, words). [P]
- Generic advice favors them but warns that vague praise ("she's great!") reads as disingenuous and recommends specific, skill-anchored quotes. ([The Muse](https://themuse.com/advice/6-things-you-should-put-on-your-personal-websiteand-6-things-to-avoid-at-all-costs)) [S]

### Resume link

- Mixed among exemplars: Chiang has a "View Full Resume" PDF link at the end of Experience; Karpathy and Huyen have none (the timeline or bio inline serves). Hiring-side advice is to link a current PDF and to keep it current, since an outdated one signals abandonment. [P/S]
- Chiang's placement (end of the experience list, as the next step for a reader who wants more) is the one concrete pattern seen.

### Guestbooks and interactive bits

- No exemplar had a guestbook. The only guidance found is operational: open guestbooks attract spam heavily (a hosting provider advises against them; Create.net discontinued theirs after spam passed reCAPTCHA; Zeldman argues AI-written spam makes it worse), and mitigation is moderation, Akismet-style filtering, or a feedback form. ([Tiger Technologies](https://support.tigertech.net/guestbook-unmoderated), [Zeldman](https://zeldman.com/2024/04/09/akismet-means-never-having-to-say-youre-sorry)) [S]
- Interactive bits that did appear are craft-adjacent: Comeau's interactive explainers (Flexbox, Grid, SVG paths), sound and theme toggles; Chiang's TARDIS GIF; Bruno Simon's 3D driving portfolio is the frequently cited extreme, aimed at creative-dev hiring. ([WeAreDevelopers roundup](https://wearedevelopers.com/magazine/web-developer-portfolio-examples)) [S]
- Hiring-side advice is against heavy animation, scroll-jacking, and hero videos. [S]

## Sorted: common, memorable, filler or risky

### Common (seen at most exemplars)

- A short bio (2 to 3 sentences) near the top; role and employer history compressed.
- Links out: GitHub, social, email; a writing destination.
- Selected writing, usually as a short dated list or a nav item.
- Curated talks, when talks exist.
- A plain, fast, minimal design (Karpathy and Willison both emphasize simplicity); dark mode on some.

### Reads as memorable

- A distinct voice in the bio (Karpathy's unicorn joke, Evans's exclamation-point topics, Kalzumeus's "recovering salaryman").
- One or two specific personal details tied to craft or identity (Huyen's story collections, Karpathy's speedcubing, Chiang's cats plus Zelda in a single sentence).
- Evidence of depth that is countable: Yan's stats row; Weng's long-form posts.
- A "Start Here" or "favourites" curation that tells a reader where to begin (Yan).
- A footer "how this site is built" line (Karpathy, Chiang, Willison's About), which engineers read as a craft signal.

### Reads as filler or oversharing (per [S] advice; no hard evidence of harm)

- Vague passion statements, mission statements, skill bars, lists of every technology.
- Self-written or generic testimonials.
- Stale blogs, "Coming Soon" sections, outdated /now or resume.
- Travel photo galleries and hobby sections unrelated to the work.
- Unmoderated guestbooks (spam load).
- Heavy animation or scroll effects that slow a 60-second read.

## Findings that bear on other tickets

- The map's constraints (little JS, Lighthouse 95/100/100/100, strict CSP, static by default) match the exemplar taste for minimal sites; the "zero frameworks" boast is itself a memorable trait.
- A guestbook needs on-demand storage and spam handling, which conflicts with static-by-default; it would be the one element in this survey that forces a runtime feature, and no exemplar has one.
- /now and /uses are cheap to build statically but carry a staleness cost; the only support found is indie-web convention, not ML-hiring norms.
- Writing is the main depth signal at ML/AI exemplars, but irregular cadence is normal; the evidence supports few strong posts over frequent ones.
- The Profile in GLOSSARY.md already holds photo and intro; nothing found argues for a long About beyond that.

## Open gaps

- No platform-engineer exemplar sites were found with a distinct pattern (SRE and infra engineers in this sample mostly publish through employer blogs or social); the sample skews to ML/AI writers.
- No first-hand recruiter or hiring-manager interviews were located; the evidence on what they read is vendor advice.
